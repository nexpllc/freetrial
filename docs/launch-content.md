# Free Trial Boyfriend — Launch Content

Everything below is written to be copied and posted. Swap `[LINK]` for your store URL and `[DATE]` for your drop date.

---

## The one thing that decides this launch

The shirt is a joke that a girl repeats to her friends. So the content isn't "here's my new brand" — it's **the joke happening to a real person on camera**. Every format below is built so the punchline lands before someone can scroll.

Your face doesn't have to be the product. The reaction does.

---

## TikTok — 10 formats

Post 2 a day for the first two weeks. One good one carries the launch; you can't predict which, so volume matters more than polish.

**1. The unboxing that reads like a subscription**
Open the mailer, pull the insert that says "your trial has started," read the terms out loud dead serious. Cut when you get to "trial renews indefinitely."
> Hook: "so I signed up for a free trial and this showed up"

**2. Girlfriend reaction**
She opens the box, reads the tag, looks at you. That look is the video.
> Hook: "she thinks it's a real subscription"

**3. Wearing it in public, no explanation**
Static shot at a grocery store, gym, Target. Just cuts of strangers reading the shirt.
> Hook: "counting how many people read my shirt out loud"

**4. Customer service bit**
You in a headset "handling cancellations." Read fake cancellation reasons off a screen.
> Hook: "day 4 of running customer support for free trial boyfriend"

**5. The terms and conditions**
Scroll a fake ToS on screen while you read it fast like a pharma ad. Land on "he will not take it well."

**6. Making the shirt**
Press, peel, hold it up. Satisfying, algorithm-friendly, no acting required.
> Hook: "making the shirt that got me in trouble"

**7. Trial expired**
You wearing the "trial expired" tee looking rough, eating cereal at 2pm.
> Hook: "she didn't renew"

**8. Comment-reply video**
Wait for one good comment, reply with a video. Free reach, zero effort, and it makes the account look alive.

**9. Two shirts, one couple**
She wears "trial expired," you wear "upgrade to premium." Just standing there. No dialogue.

**10. The pricing page bit**
Whiteboard or screen: Free tier / Premium / Enterprise, with what each includes. Enterprise is "meets the parents."

**Sound and format notes**
- Hook in the first 1.5 seconds, on screen as text, not spoken.
- Shoot vertical, natural light, no music louder than your voice.
- Do not put the link in the video. Put it in bio and say "in bio" once at the end, if at all.

## Instagram

**Grid, first 9 posts**
1. Lockup on white — the logo, nothing else
2. Product on model, front
3. The insert card ("your trial has started")
4. Meme post: fake iPhone subscription screen for "Boyfriend — Free Trial"
5. Product flat lay with hang tag visible
6. Text post: the pricing tiers
7. Reel (repost your best TikTok, no watermark)
8. Sticker pack laid out
9. "Trial expired" tee

**Caption bank**
- "no card required. [LINK]"
- "seven days. then he's yours. [DATE]"
- "cancel anytime. he will not take it well."
- "she read the tag and said 'is this real'"
- "terms and conditions apply. mostly to me."
- "free tier is generous. premium folds laundry."

**Story sequence for drop day** (7 frames, post over 2 hours)
1. Black screen: "trial starts in 3 hours"
2. Product close-up, sticker: countdown
3. Poll: "would you renew? yes / no"
4. The insert card
5. "live now" + link sticker
6. Repost first customer story
7. "day one is gone" — scarcity only if it's true

## What to watch, and what to ignore

**Watch:** add-to-cart rate, saves and shares on TikTok, replies to your story.

**Ignore:** likes, follower count, and view count on any single video. One video going wide before the store converts just means you paid for traffic in attention and got nothing back — so make sure the product page works before you push volume.

**The honest risk:** this brand is one joke. It sells hard for a few weeks and then it's a t-shirt with a dated punchline on it. Plan design 2 and 3 now, while the attention is arriving, not after it leaves. The subscription-parody frame is wide enough to carry a real line — pricing tiers, terms, renewals, downgrades — if you keep writing into it.
